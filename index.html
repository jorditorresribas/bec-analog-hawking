Hawking spectrum · quantum calculation
solver n(ω)Planck at T_H
Occupation n(ω) of the Hawking mode, computed by the solver at 40 frequencies. It is not an exact Planck curve (lower chart): the effective temperature T_ω = ω/ln(1 + 1/n) is 0.958·T_H at low frequency, peaks at 0.975·T_H around ω ≈ 5·T_H and falls to 0.89·T_H next to the dispersive cut-off ω_max ≈ 10.9·T_H, above which there is no Hawking emission.
Computed with the v2.0 solver (commit ff8625c); script and data in data/ (hawking_spectrum.py, hawking_spectrum.csv).

Sources: fp_benchmark.csv and diag_T2.csv (repo v2.0). The dashed line is T = T_H.
What each panel is. The animation is a classical (mean-field) 1D Gross-Pitaevskii simulation running live in your browser, with the same flow profile (Mach 0.5 → 2, w = 2) and the same λ as the solver. It shows how waves are trapped at the horizon and how dispersion lets short waves out; wave amplitudes are drawn ×4. It cannot show Hawking radiation, which is a quantum effect. The two charts are quantum (Bogoliubov) calculations from the validated solver, with the source of every number linked. With the corrected background, changing λ is mathematically the same as changing the width of the horizon.
What has been validatedrepo v2.0
  • Hawking temperature vs Finazzi–Parentani (2011): T/T_H = 0.957 → 0.985 for w = 2 → 8. fp_benchmark.csv

  • λ is the horizon width: (λ, w) ≡ (1, w/√λ) to 3.5×10⁻⁹ in 90 points. CORRELATIONS.md §9
